## 🎯 1. Lab Overview & Objectives

J.R.R. Tolkien was a master of **geographical narrative**—using landscape, topography, and travel logistics as central plot engines. MUME translates Tolkien's prose into thousands of interconnected text rooms mapped precisely to Middle-earth geography.

In this lab, students explore how written prose creates immersive spatial environments and analyze how MUME transforms passive literary description into active, interactive narrative space.

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lab, students will be able to:
1. Explain the concept of **spatial narrative** and environmental story design in text-based games.
2. Analyze how sensory details (sight, sound, weather, lighting) enhance immersive worldbuilding.
3. Draft an original, canonical Middle-earth room description using spatial orientation rules.

---

## 💡 2. Concept Walkthrough: Room Description Analysis

In MUME, every room acts as a miniature prose poem that communicates spatial boundaries, mood, and interactive possibilities:

```text
The Beer Garden - The Foaming Mug
A low stone wall encloses a pleasant beer garden behind the inn. Sturdy wooden
benches are shaded by ancient oak trees, whose leaves rustle softly in the gentle
breeze coming off the East Road. To the south, a heavy oak door leads back into
the warm taproom of the inn.
Exits: [South], East.
```

### Key Elements of Narrative Spatial Design:
1. **Anchor & Boundaries:** Identifies the immediate setting (`A low stone wall encloses...`).
2. **Sensory Details:** Invokes sight (`ancient oak trees`), sound (`rustle softly`), and touch (`gentle breeze`).
3. **Compass Orientations:** Connects room exit paths logically to neighboring geographical zones (`To the south... leads back into the warm taproom`).

## 🧪 3. Guided Hands-On Activity (15 Minutes)

### Task 1: Environment Inspection in MUME
1. Connect to the <a href="https://mume.org/play" target="_self" rel="external">MUME Web Client</a>.
2. Move through 3 adjacent rooms using direction commands (`n`, `s`, `e`, `w`).
3. Re-read each room description carefully using the `look` command.
4. Record how each room communicates time of day, lighting, weather, and exits.

---

## 🚀 4. Independent Student Challenge ("The Quest")

**Challenge Requirement: Draft an Original Middle-Earth Room Description**
Write an original 3-5 sentence text room description set in a canonical Middle-earth location (e.g., *The Bree Land*, *Rivendell*, *Mirkwood*, *The Old Forest*, or *Barrow-downs*).

Your room description MUST include:
* **Setting Title:** (e.g., `A Shaded Hollow in the Chetwood`)
* **Sensory Details:** At least two distinct senses (visual, auditory, tactile, or olfactory).
* **Clear Directional Exits:** Textually describe paths leading out in at least two compass directions.

---

## 📝 5. Verification & Submission Requirements

Submit your written room description along with answers to these reflection questions:

1. How does navigating a room grid in a text MUD differ from reading a passage in a novel?
2. How do room exits create suspense or influence a player's emotional state?
3. Paste your student-authored room description.
