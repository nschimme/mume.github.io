## 🎯 1. Lab Overview & Objectives

Adapting a literary masterpiece into an interactive virtual world requires fundamentally restructuring **linear passive prose** into **non-linear interactive spatial grids**. In a novel, the author dictates pacing, camera focus, and emotional timing. In a MUD like MUME, the reader becomes a player who dictates spatial navigation, agency, and pacing.

In this lab, students perform a side-by-side comparative analysis between J.R.R. Tolkien's original text passages and MUME's interactive room descriptions.

### Learning Outcomes
1. Identify the formal differences between passive literary prose and interactive spatial room descriptions.
2. Evaluate how MUME preserves canonical lore while adapting physical geography into a 3D compass grid.
3. Complete a formal **Prose-to-Grid Adaptation Matrix**.

## 💡 2. Core Theory Walkthrough: The Adaptation Matrix

Compare J.R.R. Tolkien's description of **The Prancing Pony Inn in Bree** with MUME's interactive room descriptions:

### Literary Prose (Tolkien, *The Fellowship of the Ring*):
> *"The Inn of Bree was kept by Barliman Butterbur... Even from the outside the Inn looked a pleasant house to familiar eyes. It had a front on the Road, and two wings running back on land squeezed out of the hill-side... A large archway led to the courtyard between the wings."*

### Adaptation Matrix Analysis:

| Dimension | Tolkien's Novel Prose | MUME Interactive Room Grid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Narrative Control** | Author-driven, linear, fixed camera perspective. | Player-driven, non-linear, spatial navigation. |
| **Sensory Focus** | Selective thematic details emphasizing mood. | Constant environmental anchors (lighting, weather, exits). |
| **Temporal Mechanics** | Static past tense narrative time. | Dynamic real-time ticking clock (day/night, seasons). |
| **Character Agency** | Readers observe Frodo and Aragorn. | Readers *are* active agents exploring Bree independently. |

## 🧪 3. Guided Hands-On Task (20 Minutes)

::: info 🏰 Classroom Sandbox Note
Students compare a passage from *The Fellowship of the Ring* (e.g., Rivendell, Bree, or the West Gate of Moria) with MUME room descriptions in **Bree** or **Rivendell**.
:::

### Step 1: Select a Canonical Passage
Read a short 1-paragraph passage from Tolkien describing a specific location (e.g. *The Prancing Pony Common Room*, *Rivendell Valley*, or *The Old Forest*).

### Step 2: Extract Adaptation Components
Identify how MUME translates literary elements into interactive game mechanics:
1. **Setting Anchor:** What physical boundaries are preserved?
2. **Dynamic Elements:** How are static background descriptions converted into dynamic NPCs or interactive objects?
3. **Cardinal Topology:** How are visual landscape features mapped to North/South/East/West exit directions?

---

## 🚀 4. Independent Challenge ("The Quest")

**Challenge Requirement: Complete a Comparative Adaptation Essay**

Write a 250-word adaptation critique answering:
1. What details did MUME developers *add* to Tolkien's original description to make the space functional for interactive gameplay?
2. What literary qualities are *lost* when converting passive prose into a 4-compass exit grid?
3. How does the presence of real-time day/night cycles alter the reader's relationship with Middle-earth?

---

## 📝 5. Verification & Submission Requirements

Submit your completed comparative analysis:
1. **Side-by-Side Adaptation Table:** Completed 4-row matrix contrasting novel prose with MUME terminal output.
2. **Adaptation Critique Essay:** (250+ words).
